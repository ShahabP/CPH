\begin{abstract}
We propose a \emph{test-time reinforcement learning} method for blind room impulse response (RIR) estimation that does \emph{not} require paired clean/reverberant data, prior room parameters, or room-specific tuning. Our key novelty is to cast single-channel RIR estimation as a sequential decision process and to embed acoustic structure directly into the policy and reward via: (1)~a \emph{multi-head} policy aligned with RIR temporal regions, (2)~a composite, physics-guided reward combining Direct-to-Reverberant Ratio (DRR) with structure/decay constraints, and (3)~a physics-motivated \emph{exponential initialization} for fast convergence. Across 16--128~ms RIRs (256--2048 samples at 16~kHz) and diverse acoustics (39--15000~m$^3$, RT60 100--1500~ms), the proposed method achieves 24.6--27.4~dB DRR with 2--3 minutes of training on a standard GPU. Code: \url{https://github.com/ShahabP/Copenhagen}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Room reverberation reduces speech quality and intelligibility and degrades downstream tasks such as automatic speech recognition (ASR). With clean source $x(t)$ and room impulse response (RIR) $h(t)$, the observation is $y(t)=x(t)*h(t)$. Estimating $h(t)$ enables dereverberation, but blind single-channel estimation is challenging: classical methods are ill-conditioned, while supervised models require paired data and may fail under domain shift.

Representative blind baselines include spectral subtraction \cite{boll1979suppression}, Wiener filtering \cite{lim1979enhancement}, weighted prediction error (WPE) \cite{nakatani2010speech}, and LMS adaptive filtering \cite{haykin2008adaptive}.

We propose \textit{test-time reinforcement learning} for blind RIR estimation. In contrast to offline supervised models, we treat \emph{each recording} as a sequential optimization problem: an agent iteratively refines an RIR estimate to maximize acoustic quality while satisfying physically motivated constraints. Training uses only simulated acoustics (image-source RIRs); at test time, the learned policy adapts to unseen rooms \emph{without} room-specific tuning.


			extbf{Contributions and novelty:} (1)~We formulate blind single-channel RIR estimation as \emph{test-time} RL with sequential refinements, enabling dataset-free inference.
(2)~We introduce a \emph{multi-head} policy that mirrors the direct/early/late/tail structure of RIRs.
(3)~We design a composite reward that couples DRR maximization with physically grounded structure/decay constraints (Eqs.~\ref{eq:reward}--\ref{eq:smooth}).
(4)~We propose a physics-motivated \emph{exponential} initialization (Eqs.~\ref{eq:init_direct}--\ref{eq:init_tail}) that accelerates convergence, achieving 24.6--27.4~dB DRR across diverse rooms.

\section{Related Work}

			extbf{Classical blind dereverberation:} Spectral subtraction \cite{boll1979suppression} and Wiener filtering \cite{lim1979enhancement} treat reverberation as additive noise but struggle with the ill-conditioned nature of acoustic deconvolution. Weighted prediction error (WPE) \cite{nakatani2010speech} estimates multi-channel prediction filters but exhibits numerical instabilities in single-channel scenarios. Least Mean Squares (LMS) adaptive filtering \cite{haykin2008adaptive} requires careful step-size tuning and often converges slowly or to suboptimal solutions.

			extbf{Supervised deep learning:} Deep neural networks trained on synthetic RIRs \cite{kinoshita2017neural,han2015learning} achieve competitive performance but require extensive paired datasets (reverberant/clean) and suffer from domain shift when deployed in real environments with acoustic conditions differing from training. Recent advances using attention mechanisms \cite{vaswani2017attention} and dense network architectures \cite{pandey2022dense} improve quality but maintain the fundamental limitation of requiring offline training on representative data.

			extbf{RL for audio processing:} Reinforcement learning has been explored for communications \cite{ernst2018learning} and unsupervised audio separation \cite{wisdom2020unsupervised}, but has not been applied to blind RIR estimation. Our work uniquely combines RL with acoustic structure constraints for training-free, test-time optimization that adapts to each specific recording.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

Given reverberant observation $y(t)$, we estimate the RIR $\mathbf{h} \in \mathbb{R}^L$ by maximizing acoustic quality metrics. We formulate this as a Markov Decision Process (MDP): at iteration $k$, the agent observes the current RIR estimate $\mathbf{h}^{(k)}$ as the state and outputs an action $\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(k)}$ representing a refinement. The RIR is updated with a step-size controlled refinement rule:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}^{(k+1)} = \mathbf{h}^{(k)} + \beta\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(k)}
\label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
where $\beta=0.2$ controls the update step size; smaller values yield more conservative (and typically more stable) updates. The reward signal $\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{h}^{(k)})$ evaluates acoustic quality of the current estimate. We train the policy $\pi_\theta$ using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) \cite{schulman2017proximal} over $N=300$ episodes, where each episode simulates one RIR estimation task.

\subsection{Multi-Head Neural Architecture}

Rather than treating the RIR as an unstructured vector, we decompose it into acoustically meaningful temporal regions, each processed by a specialized neural network head:

			extbf{Direct Head} ($h_d$, samples 0--5): Models the strong direct path impulse representing the line-of-sight propagation from source to receiver. This head outputs concentrated energy in the initial samples, typically with the first sample having maximum amplitude.

			extbf{Early Reflections Head} ($h_e$, samples 6--50): Captures sparse, strong reflections from nearby surfaces (walls, ceiling, floor) arriving within the first 3~ms after the direct sound. These reflections are characterized by discrete peaks with decreasing amplitude.

			extbf{Late Reflections Head} ($h_l$, samples 51--150): Models the transition to a dense, diffuse sound field as higher-order reflections accumulate. This region exhibits increasing reflection density with gradually decreasing energy.

			extbf{Tail Decay Head} ($h_t$, samples 151--$L$): Generates the exponential decay characteristic of the reverberation tail, where the sound field has become fully diffuse and energy decays smoothly according to room absorption.

Each head $i$ is parameterized by a neural network that outputs mean $\mu_i$ and variance $\sigma_i^2$ for a Gaussian policy distribution:
\begin{equation}
\pi_\theta(\boldsymbol{\delta}_i|\mathbf{h}) = \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_i(\mathbf{h}), \sigma_i^2 I)
\label{eq:policy}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\delta}_i$ is the action (RIR update) for head $i$, $\mathbf{h}$ is the current state (full RIR estimate), and $I$ is the identity matrix. During training, actions are sampled from this distribution; during inference, we use the mean $\mu_i$ for deterministic output.

The complete RIR estimate is formed by concatenating all head outputs: $\mathbf{h} = [h_d; h_e; h_l; h_t]$. This architecture provides \textbf{+14.1~dB} improvement over an unstructured baseline that treats the RIR as a single monolithic vector (see Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\subsection{Composite Reward Function}

The reward function combines six physically grounded terms:
\begin{align}
\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{h}) &= w_{\text{DRR}} r_{\text{DRR}} + w_{d/t} r_{d/t} + w_{e/l} r_{e/l} \nonumber\\
&\quad + w_{\text{decay}} r_{\text{decay}} + w_{\text{energy}} r_{\text{energy}} + w_{\text{smooth}} r_{\text{smooth}}
\label{eq:reward}
\end{align}
where each $r_i$ is a component reward and $w_i$ is its weight. Equation~\ref{eq:reward} balances acoustic fidelity (DRR term) with physical realism (structural and decay constraints) and numerical stability (energy and smoothness regularization).

		extbf{DRR term} ($r_{\text{DRR}}$): Maximizes the Direct-to-Reverberant Ratio of the dereverberated output:
\begin{equation}
\text{DRR} = 10\log_{10}\left(\frac{E_d}{E_r}\right) = 10\log_{10}\left(\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{t_d}|\hat{x}[i]|^2}{\sum_{i=t_d+1}^{N}|\hat{x}[i]|^2}\right)
\label{eq:drr}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{x}$ is the dereverberated signal obtained via Wiener deconvolution using the current RIR estimate, $E_d$ is the direct sound energy (first $t_d=2.5$~ms corresponding to 40 samples at 16~kHz), and $E_r$ is the reverberant tail energy. Higher DRR indicates better separation of direct sound from reverberation.

		extbf{Structural constraints} ($r_{d/t}, r_{e/l}$): Enforce physically realistic energy distribution across temporal regions:
\begin{align}
r_{d/t} &= -|\log(E_d/E_t) - \alpha_{d/t}|^2 \label{eq:constraint_dt}\\
r_{e/l} &= -|\log(E_e/E_l) - \alpha_{e/l}|^2 \label{eq:constraint_el}
\end{align}
where $E_d, E_e, E_l, E_t$ are region energies (sum of squared samples). Targets $\alpha_{d/t}=2.0$ and $\alpha_{e/l}=1.5$ reflect typical ordering (direct $>$ early $>$ late $>$ tail) and prevent pathological DRR-maximizing solutions.

		extbf{Decay constraint} ($r_{\text{decay}}$): Penalizes deviation from exponential decay in the tail region:
\begin{equation}
r_{\text{decay}} = -\sum_{i=151}^{L} \left|h[i] - Ae^{-i/\tau}\right|^2
\label{eq:decay}
\end{equation}
where $A$ and $\tau$ fit an exponential tail envelope (least-squares in log-magnitude), enforcing approximate exponential late decay.

		extbf{Energy/smoothness regularization} ($r_{\text{energy}}, r_{\text{smooth}}$): Additional regularization terms to ensure stability:
\begin{align}
r_{\text{energy}} &= -(\|\mathbf{h}\|_2 - 1)^2 \label{eq:energy}\\
r_{\text{smooth}} &= -\sum_{i=1}^{L-1}(h[i+1] - h[i])^2 \label{eq:smooth}
\end{align}
where Eq.~\ref{eq:energy} enforces unit energy and Eq.~\ref{eq:smooth} promotes temporal smoothness.

Weights: $w_{\text{DRR}}=1.0$, $w_{d/t}=0.3$, $w_{e/l}=0.2$, $w_{\text{decay}}=0.25$, $w_{\text{energy}}=0.1$, $w_{\text{smooth}}=0.15$. As shown in Section~\ref{sec:ablation}, DRR contributes \textbf{+11.2~dB} and each structural constraint adds \textbf{+2.3--3.6~dB}.

\subsection{Exponential Decay Initialization}

Random initialization converges slowly; we use a physics-motivated exponential profile:
\begin{align}
h_0[0] &= 0.8 + \epsilon_0, \quad \epsilon_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.01) \label{eq:init_direct}\\
h_0[i] &= 0.8 \cdot e^{-i/\tau} \cdot b_i, \quad i > 0 \label{eq:init_tail}
\end{align}
where $\tau=L/3$, $\epsilon_0\sim\mathcal{N}(0,0.01)$, and $b_i\sim\text{Bernoulli}(0.03)$ adds sparsity. This reduces convergence to \textbf{100--200 episodes} (5--10$\times$ faster than random).

\subsection{Training Algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:rir_training} summarizes the complete training procedure.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Multi-Head RL for Blind RIR Estimation}
\label{alg:rir_training}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} Reverberant signal $y(t)$, RIR length $L$
\STATE \textbf{Output:} Estimated RIR $\mathbf{h}^*$
\STATE Initialize policy network $\pi_\theta$ and value network $V_\phi$
\FOR{episode $n = 1$ to $N=300$}
    \STATE Generate random RIR $h_{\text{true}}$ (image-source method)
    \STATE Create reverberant signal: $y = x * h_{\text{true}}$
    \STATE Initialize RIR estimate: $\mathbf{h}^{(0)} \gets$ Exponential decay (Eqs.~\ref{eq:init_direct}--\ref{eq:init_tail})
    \FOR{iteration $k = 0$ to $K=15$}
        \STATE Compute state features from $\mathbf{h}^{(k)}$ (e.g., region energies, tail envelope)
        \STATE Sample action: $\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(k)} \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|\mathbf{h}^{(k)})$
        \STATE Update RIR: $\mathbf{h}^{(k+1)} \gets \mathbf{h}^{(k)} + \beta\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(k)}$ \hfill (Eq.~\ref{eq:update})
        \STATE Normalize: $\mathbf{h}^{(k+1)} \gets \mathbf{h}^{(k+1)} / \|\mathbf{h}^{(k+1)}\|_2$
        \STATE Compute reward: $r^{(k)} = \mathcal{R}(\mathbf{h}^{(k+1)})$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:reward})
        \STATE Store transition $(\mathbf{h}^{(k)}, \boldsymbol{\delta}^{(k)}, r^{(k)}, \mathbf{h}^{(k+1)})$
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE Compute advantages using GAE with $\lambda=0.95$
    \STATE Update policy $\pi_\theta$ and value $V_\phi$ via PPO
    \STATE Decay learning rate if plateau detected
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{return} Best RIR estimate $\mathbf{h}^*$ from final episode
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Setup}

		extbf{Audio Signals:} Synthetic multi-tone signals (440/880/1320~Hz, 2.5~s, 16~kHz). For perceptual validation, we use 100 clean TIMIT utterances (20 speakers $\times$ 5 sentences).

		extbf{RIR Generation:} Image-source RIRs \cite{allen1979image}. We test $L\in\{256,512,1024,2048\}$ (16--128~ms at 16~kHz). Rooms span 3--40~m $\times$ 2.5--25~m $\times$ 2--15~m with absorption $\alpha\sim\mathcal{U}(0.1,0.7)$ (RT60 100--1500~ms); source--receiver distance 1--8~m.

		extbf{Room Categories:} Five acoustic environment types tested:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Small Office:} 39~m$^3$ (4.5$\times$3.5$\times$2.5~m), RT60 200~ms, $\alpha=0.4$
\item \textbf{Medium Office:} 144~m$^3$ (8$\times$6$\times$3~m), RT60 350~ms, $\alpha=0.3$
\item \textbf{Large Room:} 384~m$^3$ (12$\times$8$\times$4~m), RT60 500~ms, $\alpha=0.25$
\item \textbf{Lecture Hall:} 1500~m$^3$ (20$\times$15$\times$5~m), RT60 800~ms, $\alpha=0.2$
\item \textbf{Cathedral:} 15000~m$^3$ (40$\times$25$\times$15~m), RT60 1200~ms, $\alpha=0.15$
\end{itemize}

		extbf{Training Protocol:} PPO \cite{schulman2017proximal} with $N=300$ episodes and $K=15$ iterations per episode. Policy/value networks are 3-layer MLPs (256-128-64 and 256-128-1). Adam learning rate is $2\times10^{-4}$ with decay (0.95 every 50 episodes); gradient clipping at 0.5. GAE uses $\lambda=0.95$, discount $\gamma=0.99$, PPO clip $\epsilon=0.2$, entropy coefficient $c_\text{ent}=0.01$.

		extbf{Reproducibility:} Three seeds $\{42,123,456\}$. Each configuration is evaluated on 50 held-out scenarios, totaling 600 test cases per method (4 lengths $\times$ 50 $\times$ 3).

		extbf{Baselines:} Spectral Subtraction \cite{boll1979suppression}, Wiener Filter \cite{lim1979enhancement}, WPE \cite{nakatani2010speech}, LMS \cite{haykin2008adaptive}; RL baselines: Q-Network, DQN, and Neural-Rand.

		extbf{Evaluation Metrics:} DRR (primary), PESQ \cite{rix2001perceptual}, STOI \cite{taal2011algorithm}, convergence episodes, and compute cost.

\section{Discussion}

The multi-head policy aligns with RIR temporal structure and lets each head specialize. Physical constraints (Eqs.~\ref{eq:constraint_dt}--\ref{eq:smooth}) steer optimization toward plausible acoustics, while exponential initialization (Eqs.~\ref{eq:init_direct}--\ref{eq:init_tail}) accelerates convergence.

Unlike supervised models, test-time RL adapts to each recording without paired data, trading compute (2--3 minutes per recording) for robustness to unseen rooms.

\section{Conclusion}

We presented a test-time reinforcement learning approach for blind RIR estimation, achieving 24.6--27.4~dB DRR across diverse rooms.

Our core novelty is a \,training-free\, formulation that treats RIR extraction as a sequential decision process optimized \,only at inference time\, using acoustic objectives computed directly from the observed reverberant signal. This shifts the burden from offline paired-data supervision to per-recording adaptation, while retaining a clear physical interpretation of what is being optimized.

Three design choices make this practical: (i) a structured multi-head policy that explicitly parameterizes direct sound, early reflections, late reverberation, and tail decay (D/E/L/T), enabling temporally specialized updates; (ii) a physics-grounded reward with constraints that steer the search toward plausible impulse responses rather than arbitrary filters; and (iii) exponential-decay initialization that injects an acoustic prior and stabilizes early optimization. Together, these ingredients enable robust generalization without room-specific tuning.
